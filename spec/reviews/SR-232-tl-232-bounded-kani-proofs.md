## Summary

TL-232 extends the single horizon `checked_add` proof to seven verified Kani harnesses and runs them in
`make kani-check` (part of `make ci`). Kani 0.68.0 with CBMC 6.11.0 verified all seven with no failed
check and every cover property satisfied. Three of the four requested proof families are delivered in a
narrower form than requested, because CBMC ran out of memory on the direct formulations; each narrowing is
stated in the harness doc comment and below.

| FND-2321 | low | Interval arithmetic is proved exactly. `node_lookahead` (extracted from `lookahead` with unchanged behavior) returns `interval.end + max(operands)` when that sum fits `u64` and the typed `ArithmeticOverflow` otherwise, for every temporal operator, every `u32` interval and every `u64` operand value. `evaluator_temporal_endpoints_full_range` proves the evaluator's span computation never overflows for any `start <= end`. | `horizon_temporal_lookahead_is_exact_or_typed_overflow`, `evaluator_temporal_endpoints_full_range` |
| FND-2322 | medium | (Superseded in part by FND-2330 on 2026-11-09.) Evaluator no-panic is NOT proved for whole formulas of depth <= 2 over traces of length <= 4. A direct proof through the recursive `Evaluator::at` exhausted CBMC memory (over 12 GB) even for one operator over a constant leaf. It is replaced by a compositional proof: with `at` stubbed, every combinator (and, or, implies, equivalent, future, globally, until, release) returns `Ok` or a typed error for every `u64` verdict time and every interval over the full `u32` range (the proof sets the span limit to 3, so wider intervals take the typed span-limit error). The stub returns an arbitrary verdict, so this proves no-panic and typed errors only, not that any verdict is correct; `cover!` shows reachability, not correctness. Recursion depth and work are bounded at run time by `max_recursion_depth` and `max_node_evaluations`, which unit tests cover but no proof does. | `evaluator_combinators_with_stubbed_at_never_panic` |

| FND-2332 | medium | TL-372, 2026-11-09 (same sampling, 8 GB abort). Wire preflight beyond ASCII is now proved for the lexer. Cause of the earlier aborts: the heap `Vec` container stack in the lexer, not the byte count; the lexer with arbitrary bytes aborted at 8251 to 8998 MB after 52 to 88 s at 4, 6 and 8 bytes (with and without string skipping split and stubbed), while the same lexer over a fixed-capacity array stack with 4 bytes took 245 MB, 6 s. `preflight` was split into the byte-ceiling and UTF-8 checks plus `lex_usage`, and the stack became a fixed array of the owner depth ceiling plus one (callers pass `effective()` limits, so behavior is unchanged; a full stack is a typed depth refusal). Results: `lexer_never_panics_on_any_short_bytes` over every byte value, 0 to 16 bytes, symbolic depth, string and field ceilings: 3 of 3 covers, 94 s, peak 2500 MB (8 bytes: 14 s, 760 MB). The existing ASCII `preflight_never_panics_on_short_input` now takes 8 s, peak 601 MB, down from 10789 MB, so the required gate harness is back under 8 GB. Fails under a reverted mutation: `self.len -= 1` in place of `saturating_sub` in the stack pop fails the lexer harness (`attempt to subtract with overflow`, 83 s). Still not proved: lexer past 16 bytes (the requested 64), `preflight` as a whole past 4 ASCII bytes (it includes standard-library UTF-8 validation), UTF-8 validation itself, and `serde_json`. The 0 to 16 byte lexer harness cannot fill the 65-slot container stack, so the full-stack depth-refusal branch is covered by reasoning (capacity is the owner depth ceiling plus one and callers pass clamped limits), not by a Kani result. | `src/wire/common.rs`, `src/wire/kani_wire.rs` |
| FND-2330 | medium | TL-372, 2026-11-09 (Darwin arm64, load average about 11, RSS of the kani process tree sampled every 2 s, abort above 8 GB). Whole-formula no-panic is now argued compositionally, not by one direct proof. Half 1 is the existing combinator harness with `at` stubbed (peak 3514 MB, 62 s; the stub now also leaves the work counters arbitrary). Half 2, `evaluator_dispatch_with_stubbed_operators_never_panics`, proves the real `at` dispatch, the `Not` recursion and the proposition lookup with every operator method stubbed to an arbitrary verdict-or-typed-error that havocs the counters: 3-node symbolic formula, traces of 0..=4 instants, symbolic time, depth, limits and counters, 6 of 6 covers, peak 3223 MB, 103 s. By induction on recursion depth (bounded by `max_recursion_depth` and the checked increment) the pair implies the recursive `at` is panic-free for every well-formed future-profile formula. The induction is an argument, not a Kani result, and the stubs are hand-written over-approximations. Kani function contracts were not used: `stub_verified` needs `#[kani::requires/ensures]` on the production functions, which means `cfg_attr(kani, ...)` in `src/future/evaluate.rs` beyond the existing confinement, and the contract on `at` would have to be verified against its own inlined body, which is the construction that exceeded 8 GB (FND-2329); this was reasoned, not measured. Each half fails under a reverted mutation: `depth + 1` in place of the checked increment in `at` fails half 2 (1 of 606 checks failed, 57 s); `time + offset` in place of `checked_add` in `future` fails half 1 (1 of 407 failed, 79 s). | `src/future/kani_evaluate.rs` |
| FND-2331 | medium | TL-372, 2026-11-09. Compound closing-prefix is proved as an operator-level lemma (FR-023 SR-020): over a verdict-table oracle shared by a prefix and the prefix extended by one instant, constrained so decided operand verdicts are unchanged, each operator keeps a decided verdict. Three harnesses by family: `closing_prefix_boolean_operator_lemma` (and, or, implies, equivalent; 4 of 4 covers, 4 s, peak 169 MB), `closing_prefix_window_operator_lemma` (future, globally, interval end <= 2; 4 of 4, 9 s, 633 MB), `closing_prefix_until_release_lemma` (until and its release negation; 4 of 4, 55 s, 2533 MB). Together with the atomic leaf proof this is the induction step of verdict stability. Not claimed: stability of the `Not` arm and of dispatch (not in the lemma), that typed errors recur on the extension (a counterexample showed the extension can decide earlier and skip a time-overflow the prefix run reached, so the first draft asserting it failed), work-limit and depth errors, intervals ending past 2, and the statement that a verdict is decided once the prefix passes `time + lookahead`. Each harness fails under a reverted mutation that turns a pending result into False at the end of the operator (3 of 3 failed: 9 s, 14 s, 69 s). | `src/future/kani_evaluate.rs` |
